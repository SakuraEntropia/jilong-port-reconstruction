# Uncertainties, access limitations and modeling choices

The scene reconstructs the **August 2019 intact gateway exterior**. Confidence is strongest for its front silhouette and facade rhythm. Precise dimensions, hidden depth and surroundings are weaker and are represented as informed artistic estimates.

## Evidence and inference boundary

| Item | What is supported | What remains uncertain / chosen |
|---|---|---|
| Target identity | Bilateral treaty pairing, photographed title and bridgehead building | No ambiguity with similarly named logistics facilities is carried into the selected target. |
| Historical state | Two dated August 2019 front sources | Small changes between those dates cannot be excluded. |
| Width and height | Repeatable image proportions | 54 m width and approximately 21.8 m height are modeling values, not verified measurements. |
| Portal | Tall outer frame and smaller road opening below inner offices | Hidden tunnel walls, exact clearance, structural thickness and plan alignment are inferred. |
| Windows | Strong visible cadence; partly concealed inner glazing strips | Perspective and occlusion make exact mullion thickness and some returns uncertain. |
| Rear/depth | Later S04 opposite elevation confirms a substantial enclosed upper volume | Exact 2019 rear signs, roof layout and minor fittings are not documented. |
| Stone and glazing | Pale warm cladding, visible seams, dark tinted glazing | Granite classification, slab construction, coatings and physical roughness are not measured. |
| Signage | Chinese front wording and emblem location | Small Tibetan text is not reliably legible; any recreated text must be labeled approximate. |
| Bridge | Reported 2019 total length/width and visible pale balustrades | Pier geometry, substructure, expansion detail and exact railing sections are estimated. |
| Terrain | Steep rocky green slopes surround the gateway | No calibrated terrain mesh, satellite footprint or measured river cross-section obtained. |
| Site furniture | Publicly visible booths, lamps, gate panels and roadway | Exact device types, internal functions and operational layout are not reconstructed. |
| Night appearance | A historical night source was identified in search | No inspected calibrated night reference; dusk lighting is an artistic presentation. |

## Epoch management

S04 is a ground-level rear/vehicle view despite the saved filename `04_aerial_2024.jpg`. It is a later-era cross-check, not aerial mapping. Its red lettering board, glass railings and sculptural lights are not copied onto the dated 2019 front. The January 2026 green temporary bridge is excluded. Reports of August/September 2026 disaster damage are used only to establish historical scope, not to alter the intact 2019 scene.

There are several bridge generations at the crossing. The older treaty's 103 × 9 m proposal, the 2019 report's 110 × 12 m concrete bridge and the 2026 temporary truss bridge must not be combined into one claimed design. Only the 2019 concrete bridge supports this scene's baseline.

## Research access limits

- Official measured elevations, as-built drawings and a complete site plan were searched for but not obtained. No scanned drawing or inferred footprint is presented as an official plan.
- Satellite/aerial leads were located, including an official 2023 aerial article, but no usable calibrated image for the 2019 footprint was obtained. The renderer's environment is not derived from satellite measurement.
- The 2015 Xinhua nighttime photograph was located through its indexed caption. A redirect loop prevented image inspection; its lighting is not reproduced as documented fact.
- Some `xizang.gov.cn` pages timed out in the web reader. Relevant indexed text is identified as such in the source index.
- The People's Daily 2023 page failed web fetching; browser access reported a certificate-name mismatch. That warning was not bypassed. The independently downloaded S04 image was inspected instead, without claiming it is an aerial.
- S03 has an accessible source page and matching caption, but no reliable photograph capture date or photographer credit was recovered.
- S04's image URL provides a dated publisher path; exact capture date and containing article were not verified. No guessed article URL is treated as a source.

## Attribution and deliverable limits

S02 is explicitly CC0. S01's Commons page has a CC BY 4.0 versus 3.0 metadata discrepancy and an outstanding license-review flag; both versions and China News Service attribution are preserved. S03/S04 lack established reuse licenses and remain research references. Photographs are not used as PBR texture maps or baked facade projections.

The national emblem relief, small inscription details, hidden roof systems and rear 2019 fittings are approximations. The scene is intended as an editable visual reconstruction suitable for refinement, rather than an engineering record. Final README and scene metadata should retain this distinction and record material departures visible during QA.

## Access recovery and aerial review addendum

The official People's Daily April 2023 article was later successfully retrieved through its public HTTP endpoint, without suppressing TLS verification or bypassing a browser warning. Its captioned drone image is now S16/local reference05. A second Xinhua drone photograph from September 2023 was retrieved from a credited public reprint as S17/local reference06. These improve vestibule/site evidence, but neither supplies a complete roof footprint or calibrated depth. Earlier statements about initial access failure remain the record of those attempts; they no longer mean that all aerial imagery is unavailable. The 16 m modeled body depth remains provisional.

## Inner tower bay visibility
Full-resolution 2019 reference 02 confirms four glazed bays per tower. Reference 01 exposes three broad bays because much of the fourth inner bay is concealed by the projecting central facade. The model's inner bay is more fully visible; equal bay spacing and uncalibrated facade depth/perspective remain an approximation. The bay count is supported, but its apparent exposure is not an exact elevation match.
