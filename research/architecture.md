# Architecture research — 吉隆口岸热索国门, 2019

Research date: 9 October 2026. Source identifiers refer to `source_index.md`. This is a photograph-based exterior reconstruction, not a measured architectural survey.

## Identity and selected historical state

The target is the Chinese gateway and joint inspection building at the Jilong–Rasuwagadhi crossing, beside 热索桥 in 热索村, 吉隆镇, 吉隆县, 日喀则市, 西藏自治区. The 2012 bilateral treaty names the crossing 吉隆—热索瓦; Nepal's 2024 joint statement uses Jilong/Keyrung-Rasuwagadhi [S05, S06]. English aliases Gyirong, Kyirong and Jilong describe the same crossing in this context.

The port includes facilities beyond this gateway. The model does not substitute the inland service platform, logistics park, or another crossing such as 樟木—科达里. The official commerce reply specifically names 热索国门 [S07].

The design baseline is August 2019: the dated CNS front frame [S01] and Amitbalani's 15 August 2019 bridge photograph [S02]. The concrete road bridge had just been rebuilt [S08, S09]. Later facade evidence [S04] supports the opposite elevation and depth only. Its red title board and modern site fittings do not override the dated front elevation. The intact scene is historical: January 2026 photographs show a replacement temporary bridge [S12], while September 2026 reporting describes disaster damage in the gateway area [S13].

## Artist's observation

| Aspect | Directly observed evidence | Modeling consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Primary silhouette | Flat, stepped rectangular roofline; raised middle; two tall side wings and wider low glazed halls [S01, S03] | Preserve the hierarchy before adding ornaments. No pitched roof on the main gate. |
| Main portal | Very tall rectangular stone frame, broad beveled reveal, and a recessed inner facade [S01] | Model the giant frame and inner volume separately. |
| True road clearance | A smaller, deeper passage remains below the inner office facade [S01, S04] | Keep an actual open tunnel; the entire tall recess is not empty sky. |
| Structural rhythm | Tall dark glazing strips flank the middle, with pale vertical piers; partially concealed inner strips [S02, S03] | Three dominant exposed bays per side plus the inner return strip where visible. |
| Inner facade | Two horizontal rows of narrow dark windows and a decorative horizontal belt [S01] | Recess actual windows into the elevated inner volume. |
| Roof and trim | Projecting stacked cornices, small repeating dentils, larger alternating white blocks [S03] | Use editable repeated components and retain their shadow lines. |
| Material transitions | Warm pale grey/pink stone panel grid; blue-green dark glass; light trim [S02, S03] | Separate stone, glazing, mullions, trim and roofing materials. Exact stone species is unknown. |
| Entrance configuration | Glazed low halls and projecting canopies at both sides of the road [S03] | Build doors and ground-floor glazing, with modest interior depth. |
| Distinctive landmarks | Centered red/gold national emblem, gold Chinese title, smaller Tibetan text [S01, S02] | Signage and emblem occupy the dated front elevation's positions. |
| Surroundings | Concrete approach, pale stone open balustrades, gate panels, small booths and solar streetlights; steep green rocky slopes [S02] | Use bridge rails and immediate site context to establish scale. Terrain remains inferred. |

The front title reads **中华人民共和国吉隆口岸**. The smaller Tibetan inscription is visible but cannot be transcribed completely from these images with confidence. Emblem relief is an artistic approximation, not a traced official vector.

## Geometry hierarchy and view strategy

The outer frame, side wings, lower halls, recessed office volume and road passage are separate masses. Broad stone piers carry the visual weight; dark glazing and shadow within the recess provide contrast. Use the bridge-facing front as the identity view, an oblique view to reveal depth, and the opposite elevation to demonstrate a complete exterior. Side/rear roof and hidden building depth are inferred from visible returns and practical circulation.

The 2019 landscape is humid, steep and heavily vegetated, with exposed fractured rock. Exact ridgelines, riverbanks, auxiliary building footprints and vegetation species were not surveyed. A dusk variation is art direction; it is not evidence of the real building's nighttime lighting.

## Research limits

No official measured elevations, complete site plan, architectural drawings, or usable calibrated satellite footprint were obtained. A 2015 night photograph was located in search results but could not be retrieved for visual comparison. Several official pages timed out or failed to fetch. These failures do not replace the four locally inspected images. See `uncertainties.md` for the specific access and interpretation limits.

## Aerial depth-review addendum

Two additional intact-site drone photographs were subsequently downloaded and inspected: S16, the People's Daily April 2023 low frontal view, and S17, Xinhua's 11 September 2023 steep elevated portal view. S17 confirms a deep vestibule between the outer frame and the recessed inner office wall. It supports the model's separate outer portal, inner volume and lower road passage. Neither image exposes both the roof footprint and a complete exterior side edge; the provisional 16 m overall body depth therefore remains an estimate, not a newly verified dimension. Their later red lettering is excluded from the dated 2019 front design.
